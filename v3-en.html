Travel plan · detox on foot · v3 · car version + camping

Car and tent: hiking all the way,
from Shabbat to Shabbat

Monday 10 August → Tuesday 8 September 2026. The e-bike fell through, so the trip changes its DNA — and for the better: rental car + camping on the middle stretch (Nijmegen → Veluwe → IJssel → Drenthe → Groningen), no car in Amsterdam. The detox turns into hiking all the way: the national parks of the Veluwe, Veluwezoom, Dwingelderveld, Sallandse and Weerribben, a Wadden island on foot (Schiermonnikoog), the forests and glacial hills of Nijmegen and the valleys of Drenthe. Bikes rented by the day where the pedaling is worth it (the white bikes of the Hoge Veluwe, Drenthe). And — the heart of it, untouched — the four Shabbatot with community, in the center within walking distance of the sjoel: Nijmegen, Zwolle, Groningen and Amsterdam. You come back with two days of cushion before Rosh Hashanah (candle-lighting: Friday 11/9).

Step 1

The ticket

The outbound is sorted: Bluebird flight to Amsterdam, with arrival on Monday 10/8 at 18:00. The return is still to be decided (TBD) — the plan is to close the trip on 8/9 with two days of cushion before Rosh Hashanah (candle-lighting Friday 11/9).

Mind the afternoon arrival

Since you land at 18:00, Monday is just train to Zuid + check-in + rest — the kosher spots are already closed. Shopping (Mouwes/Rimon) and the real start wait for Tuesday morning. Either way you reach the first Shabbat (Nijmegen, Fri 14/8) with plenty of margin.

Step 2

The car (rental ~3 weeks: Wed 12/8 → Wed 2/9)

The clean play: no car in Amsterdam (parking is brutally expensive, milieuzone) — the city is done on foot and by tram. You pick the car up only when you head out to Nijmegen and return it before coming back to Amsterdam for the last Shabbat. A compact automatic is more than enough; the route is all motorway and provincial road, no tolls (the Netherlands has almost none).

What you need to know about driving in NL

① License + IDP: bring the International Driving Permit along with your license — they may ask for it when you pick up the car. ② Parking, not access: a modern rental car meets the milieuzones of Amsterdam/Utrecht/Arnhem without any trouble; the issue is the cost of parking in the centers. Use garages and P+R (park-and-ride on the edge + transit to the center, cheap). ③ Never leave luggage in view. ④ Gas ~€2/L; budget the fuel separately. ⑤ On Shabbat the car stays parked — book lodging within walking distance of the sjoel, with parking included or a garage nearby.

Pick-up and drop-off: the easiest is a depot on the edge of Amsterdam or at Schiphol (well connected by train). You return it at Schiphol on Wed 2/9 and go into Amsterdam by train for the last Shabbat. ~21 rental days.

Step 3

Where to sleep

The rule that orders everything

Camping on road nights · center for the 4 Shabbatot. On Shabbat you don't drive or pitch/break down a tent, and you have to walk to the sjoel — so the Shabbat nights go in a hotel/apartment in the center, right by the community. The rest of the nights, camping.

  • Camping (road nights) — Dutch campgrounds are on another level. Look for boerencampings (farm camping, small and quiet) and Natuurkampeerterreinen (deep in nature — they require the Natuurkampeerkaart, ~€23/year). Concrete, verified options: Minicamping Wylerberg or Boerderijcamping Nederrijkswald (Berg en Dal, at the foot of the Duivelsberg); NK Rheden 'Veluwezoom' (in the walled garden of the Heuven estate, at the foot of the Posbank — with a barn-shelter for rainy days); NK Lheederzand or Camping Meistershof (Dwingelderveld); Minicamping De Slamme (Weerribben). The car carries the whole tent without any fuss.
  • Center, by the sjoel (the 4 Shabbat nights) — Nijmegen, Zwolle, Groningen and Amsterdam: book now, with parking/garage nearby (the car stays put from candle-lighting to Havdalah).
  • Amsterdam (no car) — near Kastelenstraat/Zuid, 2-3 nights at the start + the closing. The kosher axis within reach. (If you want camping here too: Gaasper Camping, with metro to the center.)

Gear: with a car there's no weight limit — bring the tent or buy the basics on day one at a Decathlon (there are stores in Amsterdam/Utrecht) and donate it at the end. August has ~11 days with some rain: a good tent and a sleeping bag rated for 10 °C. Rain plan B: book a trekkershut (basic camping cabin) as backup — Drenthe is full of them (Meistershof has one) and they range from bare-bones to fridge-equipped.

Day by day

You rent a bike for a day or two wherever the pedaling adds something.

The fine print

Fine logistics

Driving in the Netherlands

  • Parking: expensive in the centers; use garages and P+R on the edge (with a combined transit fare). In Amsterdam, simply no car.
  • Milieuzones (LEZ): a modern rental meets them; the problem is the cost of parking, not the access.
  • Tolls: practically none (only two tunnels, neither on this route). Gas ~€2/L.
  • Documents: license + IDP, a credit card in the driver's name for the deposit, and insurance with broad coverage (CDW/theft).

Trains (for Amsterdam and the return)

  • You return the car at Schiphol and go into Amsterdam by train (~20 min to Zuid, no time restriction for foot passengers). To get around the city and for the day-trips, OV-chipkaart / contactless at the gate.

Camping and bike by the day

  • Networks: boerencampings (farms) and Natuurkampeerterreinen (pure nature — Natuurkampeerkaart ~€23/year). Book August in advance; the best spots in the Veluwe and Drenthe go fast.
  • Gear: with a car there's no limit — bring the tent or buy it on day 1 at a Decathlon (Amsterdam/Utrecht). Sleeping bag for ~10 °C.
  • Bike by the day: the white bikes of the Hoge Veluwe are free; in Drenthe and the IJssel towns there's rental by the day (a local rijwielhandel or the station's "fietsverhuur"). No need for a 4-week rental.

Weather and daylight

  • August: high 22–23 °C, low 13 °C, ~11 days with some rain → rain jacket, waterproof hiking footwear and a tent that can take the water.
  • Candle-lighting for the first Shabbat (Nijmegen) falls at 20:44 and drops ~13 min per week down to 20:03 in Amsterdam.

Remote work

  • Libraries with free wifi all along the route: Utrecht, Deventer, Zwolle and the unmissable Forum Groningen. Amsterdam's OBA uses Publicroam (set up once).
  • An EU data eSIM as the main network (buy it before leaving).

Apps to download before leaving

  • Flitsmeister (speed cameras and road alerts) · ANWB (roadside assistance and parking) · Kasjroetlijst NIK / "Is it Kosher" · Hebcal (candle-lighting by city) · the NS app · Buienradar (the national rain radar) · bookings: hogeveluwe.nl (park + Kröller-Müller), the Giethoorn boat.
